## Whitespace Analysis

### What ADHD adults complain they CAN'T find in current apps
Synthesized from app-store reviews and review aggregators (Tier 3, with one academic source on parent uncertainty Tier 1):

1. **Apps that are themselves not overwhelming.** EndeavorRx reviews call it "way too complicated"; one adult ADHD user "deleted it within an hour" as "overstimulating." Liven reviews flag "generic and text-heavy" content where "long paragraphs with no interactive elements" overwhelm ADHD users. Pattern: ADHD apps frequently fail the ADHD UX test.
2. **Reasonable pricing.** Inflow at $200/yr drew repeated "couldn't justify the hefty fee" complaints; "7-day free trial too short" was another recurring critique. Premium ADHD coaching pricing creates affordability whitespace at the $5–10/mo tier.
3. **Dynamic re-planning when the day goes off-rails.** Motion auto-reshuffles but ADHD reviewers call this "disorienting" — they want re-planning *with the user*, not *at* the user (Tier 2/3 review syntheses).
4. **Buffer / transition time built in.** Reclaim's "Buffer Builder" (25-min gaps before/after meetings) is repeatedly singled out as the ADHD-friendly mechanic that other AI schedulers lack.
5. **Accountability / body-doubling.** Multiple ADHD app round-ups explicitly call out body-doubling as "one of the most effective ADHD management tools" — but very few apps natively offer it.
6. **Partner sync.** ADHD-marriage resources list 30+ apps for couples impacted by ADHD; no clear winner. Lunatask's "relationship tracking" feature is mentioned as a rare differentiator.
7. **Parent-app uncertainty.** Frontiers in Computer Science (2025) ParentCoach paper documents that parents are uncertain how to help kids build planning / time-management / follow-through (Tier 1).

### Gaps in current AI scheduling apps for neurodivergent users
- **Motion** — controlling, constantly rearranges, no transparency, ADHD users report disorientation (Tier 2/3).
- **Sunsama** — praised for "buffer blocks" and mindful planning, but $20/mo and aimed at neurotypical professionals (Tier 2/3).
- **Reclaim** — strong on flow protection but enterprise/professional context, not life-scheduling.
- **None** are explicitly weekly-first; most are daily-only or calendar-overlay.

### Underserved sub-segments
- ADHD parents (managing themselves *and* a household)
- ADHD university students (high need, low ability to pay $200/yr)
- ADHD + autistic ("AuDHD") adults — Tiimo serves them but with a visual-only UX
- Couples where one partner has ADHD
- Late-diagnosed women 30–50 (booming demographic in ADHD diagnosis reports)

## Expansion Paths (Phase 2 if Planny works)

### Path 1 — ADHD Teens / High-Schoolers (parent pays)
- **Why natural:** Parents already pay for Goally ($30+/mo device + sub), Joon, EndeavorRx. Parent-pays unlocks higher ARPU and pulls in family-plan economics.
- **Market proxy:** ADHD apps market USD 1.79–2.78B in 2025 (Tier 1, wide range); North America = largest region (Tier 1). US ADHD apps sub-market USD 773M (Tier 1).
- **Required product changes:** Parent dashboard, simpler UI, school-day templates, COPPA compliance, possible school partnerships.
- **Risk:** Medium — regulated audience, longer sales cycle, but proven willingness to pay.

### Path 2 — Couples with One ADHD Partner
- **Why natural:** Strong unmet need (ADHDmarriage.com lists 30+ apps, no winner). Body-doubling and partner-sync are repeatedly cited as missing features.
- **Market proxy:** No clean number found; proxy via couples-therapy apps (Lasting acquired by Talkspace 2022).
- **Required changes:** Shared plans, partner notifications, "I need help" button, non-ADHD partner mode.
- **Risk:** Medium — engagement asymmetry between partners is hard.

### Path 3 — Autistic Adults / AuDHD
- **Why natural:** Tiimo already serves this with one product line and won iPhone App of the Year 2025 — proof of market. Executive-function need is shared.
- **Market proxy:** Roughly 2% of US adults are autistic; ~85% underemployment cited (Tier 3).
- **Required changes:** Sensory-friendly mode, predictable (non-shifting) schedules, explicit transition warnings.
- **Risk:** Low–Medium — adjacent UX needs, but same monetization motion.

### Path 4 — Burnout Recovery / Chronic Illness Productivity
- **Why natural:** Same "low executive bandwidth" job-to-be-done. Mental Health Apps Market USD 8.40B 2025 → USD 18.81B 2031 (Tier 1) provides air cover.
- **Market proxy:** Long-COVID + chronic-fatigue populations + burnout — millions in US.
- **Required changes:** Energy/pacing tracking, "spoon theory" budgeting, gentler tone.
- **Risk:** Medium — clinical claims need care; user is fragile.

### Path 5 — ADHD University Students
- **Why natural:** Massive concentration of ADHD diagnoses in college population, but typically can't pay $200/yr. Underserved by Inflow/Motion pricing.
- **Market proxy:** Roughly 6M US college students with ADHD/learning differences (commonly cited industry figure; not verified in this search round).
- **Required changes:** Semester planning, syllabus import, lower price point, group/study features.
- **Risk:** Low product risk, High monetization risk (low willingness to pay; B2B via campus disability offices is slower).

### Path 6 — Non-ADHD Executive Function Sufferers (anxiety, depression, post-burnout)
- **Why natural:** ScienceDirect (2021) and PubMed (2024) confirm anxiety, depression, bipolar disorder degrade executive function (Tier 1). Same product, much larger TAM.
- **Market proxy:** Mental Health Apps Market USD 8.40B 2025 (Tier 1).
- **Required changes:** Mostly marketing/positioning — soften the ADHD branding.
- **Risk:** Low product risk, Medium positioning risk (could dilute the ADHD-first brand that wins early).

## Strategic Whitespace Recommendations

1. **Be the "weekly OS" rather than the daily planner.** Tiimo, Routinery, and Motion all anchor on the day. Planny owns the *week*, which fits ADHD adults' real planning unit (school weeks, work weeks, therapy weeks).
2. **Re-plan *with* the user, not *at* them.** The strongest negative signal against Motion is that auto-rearrangement is disorienting. Planny should make re-planning a 10-second conversational micro-interaction.
3. **Affordable middle-tier (USD 7–10/mo).** Inflow at $200/yr is too high; Calm/Headspace at ~$70/yr is the anchor. Planny can sit in between with clear value.
4. **Build native partner / body-doubling features early.** This is the single most-mentioned missing feature in ADHD app reviews and round-ups; it also opens Expansion Path 2 (couples).
5. **Be the ADHD app that ADHD users can actually use.** A "less is more" UX is itself a differentiator given the recurring "too complicated / overwhelming" complaint pattern across Inflow, EndeavorRx, Liven, Motion.

## Substitution Threat Map

### Could a generic AI assistant (ChatGPT, Gemini, Apple Intelligence) absorb Planny's use case?
**Yes, partially — but slower than founders fear.**

- **ChatGPT Scheduled Tasks** (paid, $20/mo, up to 10 scheduled actions) and **Gemini Scheduled Actions** (Nov 2025, similar) can already run recurring reminders and summaries on top of Gmail + Google Calendar (Tier 2 — Tom's Guide, TechRadar, TechBuzz).
- **Apple Intelligence + Calendar** is positioned as a generic assistant; OpenAI is integrated, but no ADHD-specific scheduling UX has shipped (Tier 2).
- **Saner.AI** is already positioning explicitly as an ADHD-aware AI assistant on top of Gmail / Google Calendar (Tier 2/3) — this is the closest "generic AI assistant goes vertical" threat.

### How long does Planny have?
- **12–24 months of clear runway** before generic assistants are good enough at *scheduling* to compete on basic task triage.
- **24–48 months** before they meaningfully address ADHD-specific UX (transitions, buffers, dopamine, gentle re-planning, non-judgmental tone).
- **Defensible long-term moats** for Planny: (a) ADHD-specific data + RLHF-style preference data on what re-planning feels good vs. shaming, (b) brand trust in a sensitive category, (c) integrations with ADHD coaching content / therapy, (d) couples / partner features, (e) clinical / insurance reimbursement path if pursued.

## Flags

**Red Flags:**
- **Tiimo's 2025 AI planner** is the single most direct competitor: it already turns tasks into a realistic schedule with time estimates, has Apple's App-of-the-Year endorsement, and serves the same audience. Planny needs a sharp wedge vs Tiimo (weekly-first? conversational re-plan? partner sync? AuDHD-only?).
- **Generic AI assistants ship scheduled-tasks features** (ChatGPT Nov 2025, Gemini Nov 2025). This compresses Planny's window for the *generic* scheduling use case.
- **High churn / "too complicated" complaints** are widespread across the ADHD app category — meaning even well-marketed apps lose users fast. Planny must measure activation + week-2 retention obsessively.
- **Analyst variance** on ADHD-apps market size (USD 0.5B – 2.78B for 2025) means investor pitches need to be careful with the headline number.

**Yellow Flags:**
- **Inflow founder reportedly emailing a reviewer to remove a negative review** (single anecdote, Tier 3) — signals that customer-trust in the ADHD-app category is fragile and reputation risk is real.
- **Pricing ceiling appears to be ~$70–100/yr** for the mass ADHD audience; $200/yr coaching tier draws backlash. Planny should not over-price.
- **Body-doubling and partner-sync are repeatedly cited as missing** — opportunity, but also means once one ADHD app ships it well, it could leapfrog the category.
- **"AI scheduler" is becoming a crowded label** (Motion, Reclaim, Sunsama, Saner, Morgen, Alfred, Hero) — Planny needs to own a specific noun, not just "AI scheduler for ADHD."

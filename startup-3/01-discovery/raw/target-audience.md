## 1. PERSONAS

### PERSONA A — "Ronen, the Forced-In Ops Manager" (PRIMARY — Tier 1)

**Demographics & role**
- Operations / production manager (sometimes co-founder or "right hand" to owner) at a **15–50 employee Israeli manufacturer** or precision/metal/electronics workshop. Revenue roughly ₪10M–₪80M.
- Age 38–55, male-skewed in industrial sectors, Hebrew-first, pragmatic, time-poor. Wears 3 hats: production, procurement, "whatever the boss needs."
- Often the *de facto* quality owner because the company has no dedicated quality manager (too small to justify one).

**The trigger (jobs-to-be-done)**
- A **defense prime / large customer / public tender** suddenly lists ISO 9001 as a תנאי סף (threshold condition). Israeli & foreign aerospace/defense manufacturers (IAI, Elbit, Rafael tier) contractually require subcontractors to hold a certified QMS; many escalate to AS9100 (which embeds ISO 9001). Source: SII.
- JTBD framed by buyer: *"Get me a certificate before the tender/PO deadline so I don't lose the deal — without me having to become a quality expert or pull my people off the line."* He is buying **a deadline met and a risk removed**, not "better quality."

**Objections / fears**
- "Will this *actually* pass the certification body audit?" (Fear of paying and still failing.)
- "An AI did this — can I trust it for something a real auditor inspects?" (Vendor distrust; buyers act as **risk managers** — research shows ~43% of B2B buyers make defensive decisions >70% of the time.)
- "How much of MY time does this eat?" (Time is the scarcest resource.)
- "Who's accountable if the auditor finds nonconformities?"
- Price sensitivity is real but **secondary to deadline + certainty** when a tender is on the line.

**Buying behavior**
- Acts fast under deadline pressure; researches via Google in Hebrew ("תקן ISO 9001 מכרז"), asks peers in WhatsApp/industry groups, may ask the certification body or accountant for a referral.
- Wants a fixed price, a clear timeline, and a name/face to hold accountable.

**Where to reach him**
- Industry WhatsApp groups; Facebook SMB/manufacturing groups; התאחדות המלאכה והתעשייה (Manufacturers & Crafts Association — represents producers up to ~20 employees) and התאחדות התעשיינים (Manufacturers Association, ~2,000 member companies / ~400k employees); SII (מכון התקנים) touchpoints; defense-prime supplier-qualification portals; accountant & bizdev referral networks.

### PERSONA B — "Galit, the Growth-Mode Owner/CEO" (SECONDARY — Tier 2)

**Demographics & role**
- Owner/CEO/founder of a **5–30 person service or light-manufacturing firm** (engineering services, logistics, contract manufacturing, food production, construction subcontractor). She is the economic buyer AND the decision-maker — in firms this size the initiator, user, and decider are often the same person.
- Revenue ₪3M–₪40M. Hands-on, allergic to bureaucracy and consultants who "sell hours."

**Trigger / JTBD**
- **Export ambition** (ISO 9001 as a credibility passport for foreign buyers; Israeli firms with ISO reportedly report higher export growth — Tier 2 claim, single source) OR a **big private customer requirement** OR positioning to qualify for future tenders proactively.
- JTBD: *"Make us look credible and 'enterprise-ready' to bigger customers and overseas buyers — cheaply and without disrupting the team."*

**Objections / fears**
- "Is this just a paper exercise that wastes my staff's time?"
- "Can a startup/AI vendor be trusted vs. an established consultant who's done it 200 times?"
- "Will I be locked into ongoing fees?" (Note: ISO 9001 requires annual surveillance audits + 3-yr recertification — recurring relationship.)

**Buying behavior**
- Slower, more discretionary than Persona A (no hard deadline). Decision shaped by trust signals, referrals, and proof. Higher CAC, longer sales cycle, but better LTV (annual surveillance attach).
- Reachable via LinkedIn (B2B-aligned), Chamber of Commerce (לשכת המסחר ת"א — Israel's largest business org, ~240 sectors), export institute / Ministry of Economy SME programs, peer founder communities.

## 2. BUYING UNIT & PROCESS

**Buying-group size scales with company size:**
- **Micro/small (5–20 ppl):** 1–2 people. Owner = initiator + decider + payer. Fast, gut-driven, trust-led. (Tier 1)
- **Small-mid (20–50 ppl):** 2–4 people. Ops/quality manager = initiator + technical evaluator; owner/CEO = economic buyer/approver; finance = sign-off. (Tier 1)
- General B2B benchmark (Gartner): 6–10 stakeholders — **but that's enterprise; deliberately discount for the SMB target here.** (Tier 2 cross-context)

**Process pattern for a compliance service:**
1. **Trigger event** (tender תנאי סף / customer PO requirement / export deal) — this is the dominant entry point per Wave 1-2 context and confirmed by Israeli sources (gov bodies — Ministry of Defense, IDF, Ministry of Health, Civil Aviation Authority — and large private firms set ISO as a threshold).
2. **Internal "can we even do this in time?" panic** → fast vendor search.
3. **Shortlist** consultants/services via referrals, Google, association/SII channels.
4. **Evaluate** on certainty-of-pass, time-to-certificate, fixed price, accountability.
5. **Approve** (owner sign-off; finance for larger SMBs).
6. **Recurring relationship** via annual surveillance audits — opens a retention/upsell motion.

**Key insight:** Low absolute spend (₪ tens of thousands) but **high strategic importance** (a deal/tender hinges on it). That profile means the buyer wants a technical specialist they can talk to — a pure self-serve AI product may hit a trust wall. (Tier 1 — matches "low spend / high strategic importance → technical-specialist-involved purchase" pattern.)

## 3. DECISION CRITERIA & OBJECTIONS

**How they choose (ranked, trigger-driven buyer):**
1. **Certainty of passing the external audit** (#1 — they are buying risk removal).
2. **Time-to-certificate** vs. their deadline (small orgs: 3–6 months typical; full project 6–12 months — the AI-native pitch is *compressing this*, the core wedge).
3. **Fixed, predictable price** (avoid open-ended consultant hours).
4. **Accountability / a human throat to choke** if the audit finds nonconformities.
5. **Minimal disruption to staff time** (their team is the bottleneck, not the documentation).
6. Referrals / social proof / track record.

**Top objections (the "what they need to hear to say yes"):**
- *"Will it pass?"* → Need a pass guarantee / re-work commitment / certification-body alignment.
- *"Can I trust an AI vendor?"* → **Verifiable trust + transparency are the real dealbreakers** for modern B2B buyers; clarity beats complexity. Lead with proof, named human oversight, references — NOT "magic AI." This directly addresses the Wave 1-2 "distrust AI vendors" finding. (Tier 1)
- *"How much of my time?"* → Quantify the time saved vs. DIY/consultant.
- *"Am I locked in?"* → Be explicit about annual surveillance (reframe as a feature: continuous readiness, not a trap).

**Pricing context (global benchmarks, USD):** consultant fees $2k–$15k+; full project $5k–$25k; consultant day rates $500–$1,250. DIY-with-prep can cut ~30% cost and ~6 months. → The DFY AI play must beat consultant price OR beat time dramatically (ideally both). (Tier 1 global; Israel pricing = **gap**, see §6.)

## 5. AUDIENCE SIZING

**Total addressable context (cross-referenced with sibling market-size.md / Wave 1-2):**
- Israel: **~600,000 active businesses; >90% are SMEs; >50% are solo/owner-only.** SMEs contribute ~55% of national output. (Tier 1 — Times of Israel / ISMEA)
- **Manufacturing enterprises ~18,000** (≈350k employees). (Tier 1 — Statista 2024)
- התאחדות התעשיינים members: **~2,000 companies** (industry + hi-tech). (Tier 1)
- **2024–25 net SMB contraction** (~50k closed vs ~46k opened, –4k net; "shockwave" warned for following year) — headwind on net new demand. (Tier 1)

**Serviceable / target estimate (SAM ≈ 2,000 active certs per Wave 1-2 context):**
- The ~2,000 active ISO 9001 cert SAM aligns roughly with the התאחדות התעשיינים member count and the upper slice of the ~18,000 manufacturers — i.e., the **realistic beachhead is a few thousand certified or about-to-be-certified firms**, concentrated in:
  - **Defense/aerospace supply chain** (highest pressure — AS9100 + ISO 9001 contractual mandate). **Tier 1 — sharpest beachhead.**
  - **Precision/metal/electronics manufacturing** (tender + prime pressure).
  - **Food & beverage** (ISO 22000/FSSC paired with 9001).
  - **Medical devices** (ISO 13485 primary; 9001 for logistics/marketing).
  - **Construction / infrastructure** (statutory + tender pressure).
  - **Engineering/ICT services & logistics** (export + big-customer credibility).

**Sectors with MOST tender/export pressure (ranked, Tier 1/2):**
1. Defense/aerospace subcontractors (contractual, non-negotiable) — Tier 1
2. Construction & infrastructure (public tenders) — Tier 1
3. Manufacturing selling to gov/primes/export — Tier 1
4. Food & beverage (export + retail-chain requirements) — Tier 2
5. Medical/med-tech (regulatory, but 13485-led) — Tier 2
